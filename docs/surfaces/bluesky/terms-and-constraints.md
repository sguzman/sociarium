# Bluesky terms and operational constraints

Research date: 2026-09-18.

This is project/technical research, not legal advice.

## Ownership

Current Bluesky Terms state that users retain ownership of the content they post.

Bluesky receives the licenses required to operate/display/distribute that content through the service.

## Decentralized persistence

The Terms also acknowledge an important consequence of the decentralized architecture:

Deleting content from Bluesky's own services does not guarantee that every copy disappears from the wider network if third-party services have received or retained it.

For Sociarium this is not an incidental bug; it is part of the protocol's distributed-data model.

## Third-party applications

The Terms distinguish the flagship Bluesky service from third-party Developer Applications built on AT Protocol.

This supports the architectural premise that Bluesky-the-company is not the sole software client or developer gateway for the network.

## Portability and identity philosophy

Current Bluesky/AT Protocol materials explicitly frame the protocol around portable identity/data and user choice among hosting/application providers.

That philosophy is materially reflected in technical mechanisms such as:

- DIDs;
- PDS migration;
- signed repository export;
- open Lexicons;
- public event streams.

## Operational constraints

Tier A does not mean "no constraints."

Relevant constraints include:

- hosted PDS rate limits;
- third-party provider limits can differ;
- private/service-specific state may not be in the public repo;
- self-hosting and rotation-key custody create operational responsibility;
- decentralized deletion cannot guarantee erasure from independent third parties;
- moderation/labeling behavior can differ among services.

## Current conclusion

No ordinary platform rule discovered in this baseline creates an X/Reddit-style contradiction with Sociarium's goal of locally preserving an authorized user's own public social record.

That is a major reason for the Tier A classification.
