### Repost representation

Three primary `TimelineTweet` entries contained a repost relation.

After unwrapping any outer `TweetWithVisibilityResults`, the reposting Post's `legacy` object contained:

`retweeted_status_result.result`

That embedded result represented the original Post.

For all three observed reposts:

- the outer reposting Post had its own stable decimal `rest_id`;
- the outer reposting Post's `rest_id` matched its own `legacy.id_str`;
- the outer reposting Post had its own `legacy.user_id_str`, identifying the reposter;
- `legacy.retweeted_status_result.result` carried a distinct original Post;
- the embedded original Post had its own stable decimal `rest_id`;
- the embedded original Post's `rest_id` matched its own `legacy.id_str`;
- the embedded original Post had its own `legacy.user_id_str`, identifying the original author.

Therefore the repost is not merely a display annotation on the original Post. The wire representation contains a distinct outer Post identity for the repost event plus a structurally embedded original Post.

### Visibility wrappers

Two of the observed reposts used direct `Tweet` values for both outer and embedded Posts.

The third demonstrated a more defensive parser case:

- the outer `tweet_results.result` was `TweetWithVisibilityResults`;
- its `.tweet` contained the reposting Post;
- `legacy.retweeted_status_result.result` for the embedded original was also `TweetWithVisibilityResults`;
- its `.tweet` contained the original Post.

A Sociarium parser therefore must normalize `TweetWithVisibilityResults` at both the primary Post position and the embedded repost-target position.

Conceptually:

```text
timeline item
  -> tweet_results.result
     -> Tweet
        OR TweetWithVisibilityResults.tweet
  -> outer Post.legacy.retweeted_status_result.result
     -> Tweet
        OR TweetWithVisibilityResults.tweet
  -> original Post
```

### Do not use `legacy.retweeted` as the relation marker

All three outer reposting Posts had a `legacy.retweeted` boolean value of `false` in this viewer's response.

The repost relation was nevertheless unambiguously present through `legacy.retweeted_status_result`.

This directly establishes an important semantic distinction:

- `legacy.retweeted_status_result` describes the Post's structural repost relationship;
- `legacy.retweeted` is viewer-relative state and must **not** be used to decide whether the Post itself is a repost.

A future parser must detect reposts structurally from the embedded result, not from the viewer-state boolean.

### Text is redundant evidence, not the parser contract

The observed outer reposting Posts also used the familiar textual `RT @...` form in `legacy.full_text`.

That text is useful as a consistency check but is not needed to recover the relationship. Sociarium should rely on `retweeted_status_result` rather than parsing display text to infer reposts.

## Confirmed baseline claims

This capture directly confirms that the current X web payload represents reposts through an outer Post containing an embedded original Post at:

`legacy.retweeted_status_result.result`

It also confirms that:

- repost outer and inner Posts retain independent stable IDs and authors;
- the already-observed `rest_id == legacy.id_str` rule holds on both layers in the three-sample repost set;
- `TweetWithVisibilityResults` can appear on either layer and must be normalized recursively;
- viewer-state `legacy.retweeted` is not the repost-type discriminator.

Together with the earlier direct reply and quote observations, the R2 reply/quote/repost representation gate is now complete.

The distinct outer Post identity strongly supports modeling a repost as a first-class relationship/event object in the normalized corpus rather than collapsing it into the original Post.
